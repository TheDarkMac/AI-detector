The storm arrived just after midnight, earlier than anyone in the village had expected. By the time Anna woke up, the wind was already rattling the shutters and rain was hammering against the windows like handfuls of gravel.

She lit a candle, since the power had gone out, and went downstairs to check on the old dog. He was lying by the door, ears pricked, staring at something she could not see.

That was when she heard it: three slow knocks.

Anna froze. Nobody lived within a mile of the farmhouse, and no one in their right mind would be walking the coast road on a night like this. She waited, holding her breath, but the knocks did not come again.

When she finally opened the door, there was no one outside. Only a small wooden box, soaked through, sitting on the doorstep with her name carved neatly into the lid.
