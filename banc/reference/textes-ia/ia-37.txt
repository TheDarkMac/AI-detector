The French Revolution began in 1789 and transformed France from an absolute monarchy into a republic. Its causes were both financial and political. The country was deeply in debt, partly because of its involvement in the American War of Independence, and the tax system placed most of the burden on the Third Estate, while the clergy and nobility enjoyed many privileges.

When King Louis XVI summoned the Estates-General to address the crisis, the representatives of the Third Estate declared themselves a National Assembly. On 14 July 1789, Parisians stormed the Bastille, a symbol of royal power.

In the following years, the Assembly abolished feudal privileges and adopted the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen. The monarchy was abolished in 1792, and the king was executed the following year.

The period known as the Terror saw thousands of executions. The Revolution ended in 1799 when Napoleon Bonaparte seized power, but its ideas of liberty and equality continued to influence the world.
